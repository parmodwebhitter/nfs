How an agency website should flow

What visitors look for, in what order, and what the best agency websites show them, so each section of our site earns its place.

The visitor's journey

Every visitor goes through four stages

A good homepage is laid out in the same order a visitor thinks. If a section answers a question the visitor hasn't asked yet, it gets skipped.

1 · Attention"Am I in the right place?"

Decided in the first few seconds, from the hero headline and the first screen.

2 · Interest"Can they solve my problem?"

Problems we solve, services, and how we work.

3 · Trust"Can I believe them?"

Results, case studies, reviews, logos, certifications, real people.

4 · Action"What do I do now, and is it safe?"

Clear offer, short form, WhatsApp / call, and risk removers.

The first screen

Five questions the hero must answer

Most visitors decide whether to stay before they scroll. The first screen should answer all five without any scrolling:

What do you do?

One plain headline about the outcome, e.g. "More leads from Google, ads and social".

Who is it for?

Name the audience: growing businesses, D2C brands, local services…

Why you?

One differentiator: results, reporting, AI search, no lock-in.

Can I trust you?

A rating line and client logos visible straight away.

What's next?

One main button or a 2-field form: "Get my free audit".

How people actually read

What visitors look at

They scan, they don't read

  • Eyes go to headings, numbers, buttons and images first
  • Short headings and bold figures get read; long paragraphs mostly don't
  • Each section should make sense from its heading alone

Most traffic is on mobile

  • Design the phone version first, then scale up
  • Tap targets at least 44px, text at least 16px
  • A sticky call / WhatsApp bar on phones

What they hunt for

  • Proof: results, reviews, logos, case studies
  • Price: at least a "starting from"
  • People: a real team and office
  • Process: what happens after they enquire
  • Contact: phone, WhatsApp, form, address

The pattern repeats: interest, then proof, then a call to action. A visitor who is ready early should never have to scroll to the bottom to act.

What strong agency sites show

Common elements on high-converting agency websites

Trust builders

  • Google / Clutch star ratings with review counts
  • Recognisable client logos
  • Case studies with before and after numbers
  • Video testimonials
  • Partner badges (Google, Meta, Shopify)
  • Team photos and founder story

Conversion elements

  • Free audit or proposal as the main offer
  • Short forms (2–4 fields)
  • Sticky header button and mobile call bar
  • WhatsApp chat (very effective in India)
  • "No lock-in", "reply in 24 hours" beside forms
  • Starting-from pricing

SEO and AI-search elements

  • A separate page for each service
  • Location pages where relevant
  • FAQ sections with schema markup
  • Regular blog with useful guides
  • Fast loading and clean HTML
  • Consistent business details everywhere online
Inner pages

How the other key pages should flow

Service page (e.g. SEO)

  1. Hero: service outcome + form
  2. Problems this service fixes
  3. What's included (deliverables)
  4. Process / timeline
  5. Results for this service
  6. Pricing / packages
  7. FAQ for this service
  8. Related services + final CTA

About page

  1. Mission in one sentence
  2. Story and years in business
  3. Team with photos and roles
  4. Values / how we work
  5. Certifications and awards
  6. Office photos and address
  7. CTA: talk to the team

Case study page

  1. Client, industry and headline result
  2. The challenge
  3. What we did
  4. Results with numbers and charts
  5. Client quote
  6. CTA: get similar results

Contact page

  1. Short form
  2. Phone, WhatsApp, email
  3. Office address and map
  4. Working hours and response time
  5. What happens after you submit
Avoid

Common mistakes that lose leads

Messaging

  • Vague headlines like "We are a creative agency"
  • Talking about the agency instead of the client's problem
  • Claims without numbers ("best", "top", "leading")

Design

  • Fast auto-rotating sliders that hide the main message
  • Too much motion, flipping and moving images
  • Low-contrast text and tiny buttons on mobile

Conversion

  • Long forms asking for budget, company size and more upfront
  • Only one contact option at the bottom of the page
  • No prices at all, so visitors leave to compare
